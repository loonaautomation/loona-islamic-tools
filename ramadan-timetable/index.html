Frequently Asked Questions

Which Ramadan year should I pick?

Enter the Hijri year (for example 1448). The tool finds the exact Gregorian dates of that Ramadan using the Umm al-Qura calendar and builds the full 29 or 30 day table for your city.

What exactly are the Sehri and Iftar columns?

Sehri ends at Fajr time and Iftar begins at Maghrib (sunset). Those are the two moments that bound the fast, so the table shows Fajr as “Sehri ends” and Maghrib as “Iftar”.

Could the real Ramadan start a day earlier or later?

Yes — Ramadan’s start is confirmed by local moon sighting in most countries, which can differ from the calculated Umm al-Qura calendar by one day. Use this timetable for planning, and follow your local announcement for the confirmed first day.

Can I print the Ramadan timetable?

Yes, the Print button produces a clean one-table layout with the city, Hijri year, method and Asr setting in the header.

How it works

Pick a city and a Hijri year and the page builds the month day by day: it steps through the Gregorian calendar, keeps every day that falls in Ramadan — month 9 — of the Umm al-Qura calendar for the year you chose, and runs the prayer engine for each one. The Sehri column is that day’s Fajr (the moment Sehri ends), the Iftar column is Maghrib, and the duration column is the gap between them. That is why a generated month has 29 rows in some years and 30 in others: the length of the calculated month itself differs.

Worked example

Ramadan 1448 in Faisalabad, with the default Karachi settings, begins on Monday 8 February 2027: Sehri ends at 5:33 AM, Iftar is at 5:48 PM, and the first fast lasts 12 hours 15 minutes. By the last day, Monday 8 March 2027, the fast has grown to 13 hours 4 minutes — and the calculated month has 29 days, with Eid al-Fitr falling on 9 March.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the Sehri column change if I change the calculation method?

Sehri is Fajr, and Fajr is fixed by the method’s twilight angle, so a different method moves every Sehri in the table. Iftar is sunset, which the method does not govern, so the Iftar column stays the same — the fast simply starts earlier or later.

Can I build the timetable for a city that is not in the list?

Yes — like the other Loona prayer tools, the page accepts manual latitude and longitude (or your device location), and the whole month is then calculated for that exact place, in that place’s time.

Ramadan has already started — can I still use this table?

Yes. Generate the current Hijri year and today’s row is in the table with its Gregorian date beside it. For just today’s two times with a live countdown, the Sehri & Iftar Today page is quicker.

Do the times in the table match the Prayer Times page?

Yes — both pages run the same engine with the same saved settings, so the Fajr and Maghrib in any row here are exactly the Fajr and Maghrib the Prayer Times page shows for that date and city.

Why does the fasting duration grow or shrink through the month?

Because the two ends of the fast move at different speeds. In the Faisalabad 1448 table, Fajr moves 27 minutes earlier across the month while Maghrib moves 22 minutes later, so the fast lengthens from 12 hours 15 minutes to 13 hours 4 minutes. In a summer Ramadan at northern latitudes the change is far bigger.
