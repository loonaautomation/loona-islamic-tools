How these times are calculated

Every time on this page is calculated, not copied from a printed table. The built-in prayer engine takes Batna's latitude and longitude plus the date, and works out the sun's path for that day: Dhuhr is the moment the sun reaches its highest point (solar noon), Sunrise and Maghrib are when the sun sits 0.833° below the horizon, Fajr and Isha are when it reaches the twilight angle of the method — 18° below the horizon for the Muslim World League Fajr — and Asr is found from the shadow factor of the school (1 for Shafi, 2 for Hanafi). Where summer twilight never fully ends at far-northern latitudes, an angle-based rule caps Fajr and Isha at a share of the night. The Hijri date is read from the Umm al-Qura calendar built into your browser. All of it runs in your browser; nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which calculation method is used for prayer times in Batna?

This page uses the Muslim World League method (Fajr 18 degrees, Isha 17 degrees) with Shafi Asr. The Muslim World League convention is the most widely used international default, and the one most often adopted where no national authority publishes its own angles. To use a different method - ISNA, Muslim World League, Egyptian General Authority or Umm al-Qura - open the main Prayer Times tool and change the method there.

Is the Asr time in Batna Hanafi or Shafi on this page?

Shafi. The Shafi, Maliki and Hanbali schools start Asr when an object's shadow equals its length plus its noon shadow (factor 1), which is the earlier of the two common Asr times. The Hanafi school uses factor 2, giving a later Asr. If you follow the Hanafi school, open the main Prayer Times tool and switch the Asr setting to Hanafi.

What is the Qibla direction from Batna?

The Qibla bearing from Batna is 106.6 degrees clockwise from true north, and the straight-line distance to the Kaaba in Makkah is about 3,621 km. It is the initial bearing of the great-circle (shortest) path to the Kaaba at 21.4225 N, 39.8262 E.

Why do these times differ by a few minutes from my local masjid in Batna?

Three reasons cover almost every difference: the masjid may use a different calculation method or twilight angles (see the first question), it may add precautionary minutes to the calculated times, and these times are computed for the city-center coordinates, while your neighborhood can sit a minute or so either side. Matching the method and Asr school removes nearly all of the gap.

How are the Batna prayer times calculated?

They are calculated fresh on your device from the sun's position for Batna's coordinates (35.5560° N, 6.1741° E) on the date shown - not copied from a printed table. Dhuhr is solar noon, Sunrise and Maghrib are when the sun is 0.833 degrees below the horizon, Fajr and Isha use the Muslim World League twilight angles (fajr 18 degrees, isha 17 degrees) and Asr uses the Shafi shadow factor of 1. The Hijri date shown uses the Umm al-Qura calendar; a local moon-sighting announcement can differ from it by a day.
